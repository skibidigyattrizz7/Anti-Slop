The chalkboard by the gate still said 11. Tunde had written it last night in his square capitals, and under it, smaller, B4 and K7 in the bad column. Nine on the road, if Ade's cousin came with the spanner and nobody asked him for money first.

Bisi came through the gate at ten past five with a flask under her arm and her wrapper knotted at the hip. She did not look at the board. She looked at B7, parked nearest the office, which had been hers for six months and was washed. The windscreen was still beaded.

"Good morning," Tunde said.

"Is it?" She set the flask on the bonnet of B4, which was not hers, which was broken, and which she had clearly decided to ignore. "The mechanic says Friday."

"He said Friday last week."

"Then he is consistent."

Outside, the first danfo of the day was already leaning into the road with its horn going, a radio inside it playing highlife with the volume turned up to beat the engine. A boy with a bucket went along the line rinsing windscreens. The air smelled of diesel and of the plantain someone was frying two stalls down.

Tunde took the envelope out of the drawer. He had put it there at four, before he could think about it. Three weeks. He had counted it twice, and then once more in case the number had changed while he was not looking. He was fifty-two, and his knees had opinions about the stairs to the office.

"I have not got it all," he said.

"I know."

"I have most of it."

"You have what the owner gave you, and the owner gave you the money for the new tyres." She unscrewed the flask and looked into it as if it might say something. "I am not saying that is wrong. Tyres are also a thing."

"Bisi."

"I drive B7 at six. I need to tell my landlord something by Saturday."

He held out the envelope. It was thinner than she would expect, and she could see that from where she stood. She did not reach for it.

"Put it on the desk," she said. "I'll see what it looks like when I'm not standing here."

He put it on the desk. The boy with the bucket came back and knocked the gate twice. Somewhere a bus started, then another. Tunde picked up the chalk and wrote 9 on the board, crossed it out, and wrote 10, and did not know which of the two was true.
