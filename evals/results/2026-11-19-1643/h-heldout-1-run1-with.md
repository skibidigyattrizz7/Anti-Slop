Nothing is wrong with you. Most people who start running get out of breath within a couple of minutes, especially if they haven't exercised much lately. Your breathing and legs adapt over a few weeks of regular practice.

Run slower than feels right. A good check is the talk test: you should be able to say a short sentence without gasping. If you can't, slow down or walk.

Try intervals instead of running continuously. Jog for one minute, walk for two, and repeat for about 20 minutes, three times a week. Over four to six weeks, shift the balance toward running until you can go longer without stopping.

See a doctor if you get chest pain or tightness, dizziness or fainting, or breathing that stays unusually hard after several weeks of training. Also mention it if you wheeze when you exercise, since asthma is common and easy to manage once it's diagnosed.
