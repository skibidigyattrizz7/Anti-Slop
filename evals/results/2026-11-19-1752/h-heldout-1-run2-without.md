Probably not. Feeling out of breath after 2 minutes is very common when you're just starting out. Running uses your heart and lungs in a way they're not used to yet, and they get better with practice, usually within a few weeks of steady effort.

A few things help:

- **Walk-run intervals.** Try 1 minute of running, then 1 to 2 minutes of walking, and repeat for 20 to 30 minutes. Over time, shift toward longer running segments and shorter walks.
- **Slow down.** Your pace should be easy enough that you could talk in short sentences. If you can't talk at all, slow down or walk more.
- **Run 3 times a week** with a rest day between sessions. Your body adapts during recovery, not just during the run.
- **Breathe steadily.** Try a rhythm like breathing in for 2 steps and out for 2 steps. Breathing through your nose or mouth both work.
- **Warm up** with 5 minutes of brisk walking before you run.

See a doctor before or while you train if you notice any of these: chest pain or pressure, dizziness, fainting or nearly fainting, a racing or irregular heartbeat that doesn't settle, wheezing or coughing that doesn't stop, or unusual tiredness that lasts for days. Also mention it if anyone in your family has had heart problems at a young age, or if you have asthma. These are usually easy to check, and getting checked is never a waste of time.

If none of those apply, keep going. Most people who start running feel this same breathlessness at first, and it gets easier with consistent practice.
