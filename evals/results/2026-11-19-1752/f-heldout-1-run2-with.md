At two in the morning the end dryer had a sign taped to it, OUT in Fiona's capitals, and under that, smaller, *ask me*. Dev read it twice before he dumped his bag on the folding table.

"Three of them now," he said. "Number four's spinning like it's got a grudge."

"Number four's always had a grudge." She was on her knees behind number six with a torch in her teeth. She took it out to talk. "Engineer's coming Thursday. Maybe."

"Thursday's no good. Cathcart's flat has the boiler man, and I'm on lates."

"Then bring it Wednesday."

He tipped his bag out. Work trousers, a school jumper, two small grey socks that were his daughter's and not his. He sorted them by colour, slowly, the way he did everything after midnight.

The surveyor's van had been outside at six. She'd watched from the window with the sale papers under her arm. A man with a tape measure had walked the length of the shop counting machines. He wrote eight. Then he looked at the three dead ones and wrote nothing.

"You're quiet," Dev said.

"I'm always quiet at two."

"You're never quiet. You've been humming Runrig since I came in."

She stopped. Machine six clicked and gave up. Something in her stomach dropped, and she let it, because Dev was busy with his towels.

"Dev. If somebody was going to buy this place. Say, by Friday."

"Is somebody buying it?"

"Somebody's looking at it."

"Good." He lifted a towel from the pile and shook it out. "You need out. You're here every hour God sends."

She watched him fold the towel in half, then in half again, and set it on the pile.

"Aye," she said. "Right."
