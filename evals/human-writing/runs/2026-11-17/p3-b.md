The night before our last competition, the robot arm dropped the cube every third try. That is a rate you can count on, which makes it the worst kind of problem. It was too frequent to ignore and too regular to trust.

At 11 p.m., one team member took the claw apart. Inside was one bent screw. Just one, and it was easy to miss.

A cube that drops every third try looks like a software bug, so that is where a team naturally looks first. The hardware gets skipped until someone thinks to check it.

We placed 4th out of 22 teams. Given the night before, we are proud of that result, and we know the claw check should have come earlier.

Our lessons are simple. Check the hardware a week before a competition, not the night before. Look at the parts, not only the code. And when a problem comes and goes, treat it as physical until you have ruled that out.
