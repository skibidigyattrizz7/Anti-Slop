# Why Is the Sky Blue?

Have you ever looked up on a clear day and wondered why the sky is blue? The answer has to do with sunlight and the tiny particles that fill our atmosphere.

Sunlight looks white, but it is really a mixture of all the colors of the rainbow. Each color travels as a wave, and each wave has a different length. Red light has long waves, while blue light has short ones.

When sunlight enters the atmosphere, it bumps into gas molecules, mostly nitrogen and oxygen. These molecules are much smaller than the wavelength of visible light, so they scatter the light in all directions. Scientists call this effect Rayleigh scattering, after the British physicist Lord Rayleigh who described it in the 1870s.

Short blue waves scatter far more strongly than long red waves. As blue light bounces around the sky, it reaches our eyes from every direction, so the whole sky appears blue.

Why not violet, which has an even shorter wavelength? There are a few reasons. The sun gives off less violet light than blue, our eyes are more sensitive to blue, and some violet light is absorbed high in the atmosphere.

At sunrise and sunset, sunlight passes through much more air before it reaches us. Most of the blue is scattered away, leaving reds and oranges to dominate the sky.

So the next time you see a blue sky, remember that you are looking at sunlight that has been scattered by billions of invisible air molecules.
