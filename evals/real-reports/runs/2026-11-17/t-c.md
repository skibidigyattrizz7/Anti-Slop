# Board brief: move from monthly to annual billing?

**Recommendation:** Test annual billing before switching everyone. Offer annual plans to new customers and to a defined group of current monthly customers for two quarters, then decide on a full switch using churn results from that test. Our data shows a large churn gap, but it does not yet show that annual billing causes lower churn.

## The evidence

- Plan mix: 4,200 customers, 31% on annual plans and 69% on monthly plans [source: your billing export, Oct 2026]. Arithmetic: 0.31 × 4,200 = 1,302 annual; 4,200 − 1,302 = 2,898 monthly.
- Last year's churn was 6.8% for monthly-plan customers and 2.1% for annual-plan customers [source: your billing export, Oct 2026].
- Caution: customers who choose annual plans may already be less likely to leave. The gap may partly reflect who chooses annual, not the plan itself. This is the main reason to test before switching.

## What the decision costs

- Revenue impact: not measured. We do not have prices, average revenue per customer or the discount we would offer, so we cannot estimate the effect on income.
- Proposed owner: finance lead (proposed, not yet agreed).
- Proposed timing: approve a two-quarter test this quarter, with a review at the end of the test.

## What would change the recommendation

- If the test group's churn falls clearly below the control group's, move to a full switch with the discount that worked.
- If churn does not fall once we control for customer type, keep monthly as the default and focus on retention for monthly customers.
- If the discount needed to win annual sign-ups wipes out the saving from lower churn, do not switch.

## Decision requested from the board

Approve a two-quarter test of annual billing, with the test design, discount level and success measure to come back to the board.
