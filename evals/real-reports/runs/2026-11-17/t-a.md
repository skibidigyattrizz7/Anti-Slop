# Board brief: Should we switch from monthly to annual billing?

**Decision requested:** Approve a test of moving customers toward annual billing, with a full switch to follow only if the test supports it.

## Where we are
- 4,200 customers.
- 31% are on annual billing. 69% are on monthly billing.
- Last year's churn was 6.8% for monthly-billed customers and 2.1% for annual-billed customers.

## Why it matters
Annual customers left at about a third of the rate of monthly customers (2.1% vs 6.8%). Every customer we keep is revenue we do not have to replace, and annual contracts make revenue more predictable.

Two cautions before we rely on this gap:
- **The comparison may not be like-for-like.** The monthly figure is a monthly rate. The annual figure may be a yearly rate. We need to confirm both are measured the same way.
- **Customers who choose annual may already be more committed.** Part of the gap may come from who picks annual, not from annual billing itself. A test is the only way to separate the two.

## Risks
- Moving customers to annual may need a discount, which lowers revenue per customer. This brief does not model that cost.
- Some monthly customers may leave rather than commit to a year.
- Annual billing can change cash timing, since customers pay up front.

## Recommendation
Move toward annual billing in stages, starting with a test:
1. Offer annual as the default choice for new customers. Offer existing monthly customers a switch with a modest discount. Set the discount level in the test.
2. Run the test for one quarter on a defined group of customers. Track switch rate to annual, churn by billing plan, and revenue per customer.
3. Before any full rollout, confirm the churn comparison by matching customers on start date and type, and model the discount cost against the revenue we keep.

**Board ask:** Approve the test and a discount budget. Decide on a full switch after the test results.
