# Board brief: move from monthly to annual billing?

**Recommendation:** Do not switch the whole base yet. Run a 12-month pilot that offers annual billing to one group of monthly customers, and decide on a full switch once the churn figures are checked and the discount cost is known.

**Decision needed:** Approve the pilot and a discount budget of [figure needed], by [date needed]. Owner: [name needed].

## Finding

Customers on annual billing churned far less last year: 2.1% against 6.8% a month for monthly billing [1]. Strength: moderate. It is one company's billing export, and I have not opened that export (see Sources).

## What the numbers mean

- Base: 4,200 customers. 31% (about 1,302) are on annual billing and 69% (about 2,898) on monthly [1].
- Monthly billing at 6.8% a month compounds to about 57% of monthly customers lost in a year (1 - 0.932^12) [1].
- The annual rate of 2.1% has no period confirmed [1]. If it is also monthly, annual customers lose about 22% a year (1 - 0.979^12), which is a much smaller gap than the headline suggests. If it is the share lost at renewal, the comparison holds.

## Caveats

- **Units:** confirm whether 2.1% is a monthly or a yearly rate before the board sees the comparison.
- **Who chooses annual:** customers who pick annual may already be more loyal. Some of the gap would appear with or without a switch. Not measured.
- **Cost:** the discount size, cash timing and refund terms are not in the facts. Not measured.

## Options

| Option | What it means | Cost | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Stay monthly | No change | None | Monthly churn stays at 6.8% |
| B. Offer annual to all monthly customers now | Full switch with a discount | Discount on about 2,898 accounts, [figure needed] | Discount may be paid by customers who would have stayed |
| C. 12-month pilot with one group (recommended) | Offer annual to a set group, compare churn with a control group | Discount on the pilot group only, [figure needed] | Slower answer; small group |

## Still unknown

1. Whether the 2.1% and 6.8% rates use the same period.
2. The cost of the discount and its effect on revenue.
3. Whether annual customers differ from monthly ones in ways that cause the gap.
