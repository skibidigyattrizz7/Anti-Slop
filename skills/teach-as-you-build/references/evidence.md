## Why explanations are optional and default to off

- Experts lose from extra explanation, and techniques that help novices can turn negative for them (expertise reversal). Kalyuga, Ayres, Chandler, Sweller 2003, Educational Psychologist 38(1). [unverified] (snippet: https://ro.uow.edu.au/edupapers/136)
- Students with an unguarded chatbot did 17 percent worse once it was removed. One school in Turkey, about 1,000 students. A tutor with guardrails largely removed the harm. Bastani et al., PNAS 2025. [unverified] (snippet: https://chibe.upenn.edu/publications/generative-ai-without-guardrails-can-harm-learning-evidence-from-high-school-mathematics)
- Khanmigo's forced questions led many students to stop using it. Press report of a draft paper. [unverified] (https://hechingerreport.org/proof-points-khanmigo-math-ai-tutor/)

## Why the explanation sits next to the result

- In a trial of 52 mostly junior engineers learning a library, the AI group scored 50 percent against 67 percent by hand. The groups that asked for explanations or concepts scored higher, but the cells were 2 to 7 people and the authors say the pattern is not causal. Anthropic, Jan 2026. [verified: https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills]
- Claude Code's Explanatory and Learning styles add insight blocks beside the code and are longer by design. [verified: https://code.claude.com/docs/en/output-styles]

## Why show the work, then fade

- Novices learn more from worked examples than from solving cold. Sweller and Cooper 1985. [unverified]
- Removing steps one by one, with self-explanation, improved learning in geometry. Renkl and Atkinson. [unverified] (snippet: https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s11251-009-9107-8)
- Model, coach, scaffold, fade is the frame, not proof. Collins, Brown, Holum 1991. [unverified] (https://www.aft.org/node/18441)

## Why one skippable question

- Retrieval beat restudy after two days and a week, not after five minutes. Roediger and Karpicke 2006, Psychological Science 17(3). [unverified] (snippet: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/)
- ChatGPT users improved essays but showed fewer metacognitive steps. Fan et al., BJET 2025. [unverified] (https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.09315v1)
- A pedagogy-built tutor beat class time in one Harvard physics trial: 194 students, two topics, short-term. Kestin et al., Scientific Reports 2025. [unverified] (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12179260/)
