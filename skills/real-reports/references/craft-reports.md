# Craft: reports, research and analysis

Classic principles below are established practice, not new findings.

## Before drafting

- Write the decision or question the reader has. One sentence.
- List what you need to know and what source type answers it. Primary first: the paper, dataset, filing, official page.
- Ask the user for facts you cannot find. Never fill the gap.

## Research and citation workflow

1. Search. Open the page. Do not trust the snippet.
2. Find the passage. Copy exact words for quotes; paraphrase for everything else.
3. Note: who, date, sample or scope, what it found, what it did not.
4. Check the claim you will write against that note. Downgrade it if needed ("one survey of 1,200 teens", not "research shows").
5. Link it in the text, next to the claim. A bare homepage link does not count.
6. If you cannot open it, do not cite it. Write "unverified" or leave it out.
7. Unfamiliar framework, product or term: search for an independent trace first.

## Structure (established practice)

- **Answer first.** Minto's Pyramid Principle (1987): conclusion first, then grouped support.
- **SCQA opening** for a memo: situation, complication, question, answer.
- **Decide, do not survey.** End with a recommendation, options, cost and the decision needed by when. A summary with no recommendation is unfinished.
- **Order by the reader's question**, not by the template.

## Numbers

- Unit, baseline, date and source every time.
- Keep the source's precision. If it gave a range, give a range.
- Recompute totals and percentages from rows. Check periods match.
- Correlation stays correlation. Say so once.
- Report the n and who was sampled.

## Uncertainty and disagreement

- One clear statement of what is strong, weak and missing.
- When sources conflict, say so, then say which you trust and why (method, sample, recency).
- Mark claims strong, moderate or weak when stakes are high.

## Editing pass

- Replace "highlights the importance of" with the so-what: what changes, for whom, by how much.
- Delete any sentence that changes neither what the reader believes nor does.
- Read the section headings alone. Do they carry the argument?

## Longer before and after

**Before:**

> The renewable energy sector is growing rapidly, and experts agree that solar power plays a crucial role. Research suggests costs have fallen significantly, highlighting the importance of continued investment. Overall, the future outlook is positive.

**After:**

> Recommendation: keep the solar tender open and add a battery option. [Source: the utility's 2025 tender summary, opened 17 Nov, p. 4; figures to be filled from that page.] The tender's three bids differ by [figure needed] per MWh. The summary reports bids only, with no maintenance costs, so the lowest bid may not be the cheapest over 20 years. Decision needed from the board by [date needed]. Not yet verified: grid connection cost.

The after version leads with the decision, points to one opened source, names a gap, and leaves visible placeholders where facts are missing.
