# Human Fiction

A model drifts to the average story: the same names, a lighthouse or a shop, one clean plot line, a feeling explained, a gentle lesson at the end. Studies of tens of thousands of stories found this across models. Skilled writers start from a specific odd thing and let the cost fall on someone. Fix the shape first; new names alone will not do.

## Top rules

1. **Start from a specific thing, not a theme.** An object, an odd job, a bad decision, a strange rule. "A night-shift crossing guard on a road with no kids."
2. **Pick names for the place, era and family.** Ask "who names their kid this?" Avoid the stock set in `references/tells.md`.
3. **Give people wants that collide.** Each wants something concrete more than being nice.
4. **Write dialogue about wants.** People interrupt and dodge. No one explains the plot or theme aloud.
5. **Add a second thread or a time jump.** One clean line of plot is the default.
6. **Make it cost something and end off-balance.** Unresolved, ironic or slightly wrong. No stated lesson, no quiet acceptance.
7. **Name a feeling plainly now and then.** Not every emotion through a racing heart or tight chest.

## Never

No brief overrides these.

- Invent facts, quotes, sources or real events and present them as real.
- Swap a flagged phrase for a synonym. Rewrite the beat.
- Tune a story to beat an AI detector, or hide AI use where it is not allowed.

## Defaults

A brief or the user's own style overrides these.

- Allow humor and mess. Each scene raises the stakes.
- Worldbuilding: three odd rules that cause problems. NPC lines are short, with a quirk, and never a lore paragraph.
- Open on the scene, not weather. Vary the shape: letters, a list.

## Before and after

**Before:** "In a quiet harbor town, a baker named Mara felt a warmth in her chest. She realized that kindness is the real treasure."

**After:** "Ines left the bread on the Hendersons' step again, and again the Hendersons left the empty tray on hers, washed, with a note about the price of flour."

**Before:** "'I'm so afraid of losing you,' he said. 'Fear is just love in disguise.'"

**After:** "'You took the good umbrella,' he said. She had. She held it out, and he didn't take it."

## Final check

- Could names and setting be swapped with no loss? Then it is generic.
- Does any line state the theme or explain a feeling already shown?
- Does the ending cost someone something, or does it tie a bow?
