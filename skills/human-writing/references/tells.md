# Writing tells (dated)

last_verified: 2026-10-22 for word lists. Structure section cross-checked against `research_notes/` on 2026-11-17.
Re-check: word and punctuation tells monthly, structure tells every 6 months, everything after a major model release.

A tell is a reason to rewrite the sentence. It is never proof of anything. One tell means nothing; several different kinds together mean the draft is running on defaults.

## Structure (strongest, slowest to expire)

| Tell | Fix | Pattern file |
|---|---|---|
| No checkable specifics: no names, numbers, dates or real examples | Add one from the user or a source, or ask | `craft.md` |
| "It's not X, it's Y", "not only X but also Y", "rather than X" | State Y | `patterns-a-staging.md` §1 |
| One-line closer that repeats the paragraph | Cut it, or make it carry a new fact | `patterns-a-staging.md` §2 |
| Warm-up before the point ("Here's the thing", restating the question) | Start on the point | `patterns-a-staging.md` §4 |
| Lists of exactly three by habit | Use the real count | `patterns-b-rhythm.md` §6 |
| Even sentence length and tidy same-size paragraphs | Break one, join two, add one short | `patterns-b-rhythm.md` |
| "-ing" tail that adds fake insight ("..., ensuring a seamless experience") | Cut it or state the effect as its own claim | `patterns-c-inflation.md` §15 |
| Ordinary things called pivotal, a testament, a turning point | Say what happened | `patterns-c-inflation.md` §13 |
| "Studies show", "experts say" with no name | Name the source or drop the claim | `patterns-c-inflation.md` §17 |
| Summary ending ("In conclusion", "Overall", "Ultimately") | Delete the last paragraph and see if anything is lost | `patterns-a-staging.md` §2 |
| Bold on every term, headings on a short answer, bullets for prose | Format for the destination | `patterns-d-formatting.md` |
| "Great question", "I hope this helps", offers of more help | Delete | `patterns-e-leftovers.md` §22 |

## Words and punctuation (expire fast)

Count these only in clusters of three or more.

- **Current:** crucial, pivotal, underscore, significant, seamless, robust, elevate, unlock, leverage, foster, vibrant, transformative, realm, testament, landscape (abstract use).
- **Newer, Claude-leaning:** genuinely, quietly, honestly, load-bearing, "doing real work", "stated plainly".
- **Em dashes:** above about 3 per 1,000 words reads as a model habit for some models. Weak sign. Many people use them.
- **Expired as tells:** delve, tapestry, intricate, showcasing. Still plain-word them, but they show nothing on their own.
- **Stock phrases:** "in today's fast-paced world", "it's important to note", "it's worth mentioning", "when it comes to", "at the end of the day".

## Guards

- Formal, academic, simple or non-native English is not AI writing. Lower your confidence for these.
- A quotation, a title or a passage that discusses a phrase is exempt.
- People score near chance at spotting AI text by feel. Judge the writing, never the writer.
- English lists do not transfer to other languages.
