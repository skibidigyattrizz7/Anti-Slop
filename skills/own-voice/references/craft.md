# Own voice craft

## Getting the material

Ask one short question at a time. Ask for things that can be pointed at.

- "What did the room look like?" beats "What did you learn?"
- "What number would you put on it: hours, people, money?"
- "What did you say, word for word, if you remember?"
- If the user has nothing, say so and stop. Do not fill the gap. A short true piece beats a long invented one.

## Using a sample

Read their sample for four things: average sentence length, how they open, what they never write (long words, semicolons, "moreover"), and one quirk (starts with "So", uses brackets, drops pronouns). Match those four, nothing else.

## Editing, not rewriting

- Fix clarity and grammar first.
- Cut one thing that is repeated.
- If a sentence is rough but sounds like them, leave it.
- Never make three sentences into one smooth one. Smoothness is the tell.
- Show what you changed in a line or two so they can say no.

## By format

- **Email or message:** greeting, the ask in the first two lines, the detail needed to act, a sign-off. Under 120 words for most.
- **Resume bullet:** verb, thing, result. "Cut check-in time from 6 to 4 minutes by rebuilding the sign-in sheet" only if the user gave 6 and 4.
- **Cover letter:** one reason this role, one real example, one line on what they would do first.
- **Personal statement:** one small true scene early, honest reflection in plain words, no claim bigger than the scene.
- **Speech:** short sentences, one story, repeat the key phrase once on purpose, end on the story.
- **Bio:** third person only if asked. One odd, specific fact.

## High-stakes work

For applications, graded work or contests: ask what the rules say about AI help. If it is banned or unknown, ask questions, give an outline, and comment on the user's draft. Do not write the piece.

## Longer before and after

**Before (stock):** "Throughout my time on the robotics team, I developed strong leadership skills and learned the importance of perseverance. This experience taught me that challenges are opportunities for growth."

**Facts the user gave:** captain for one season; the claw dropped the cube every third try the night before regionals; found one bent screw at 11 p.m.; placed 4th of 22.

**After:** "I was captain for one season. The night before regionals our claw dropped the cube every third try. At 11 p.m. I took it apart on the garage floor and found one bent screw. We placed 4th out of 22."

What changed: the claims became events, every fact is the user's, and the ending is a result, not a moral.
